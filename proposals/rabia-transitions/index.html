The case for change

Your work is extraordinary.
Your visibility isn't.

Rabia Transitions has spent five years building something genuinely rare — rigorous, applied, country-specific climate finance research that sits at exactly the intersection where global frameworks meet African economic reality. The Climate Risk Indicators framework. The Country Transition Risk Model. The Just Term Sheet. Five years of work that central banks, finance ministries, and financial institutions need but cannot find.

The problem, as your own strategic documents identify, is not the quality of the work. It is translation. The people who need to act on your research are not reading 80-page technical reports. They are scrolling LinkedIn between meetings, looking for the authoritative signal in a sea of generic climate content.

"The core challenge is not the quality of the work, but its translation — how to surface, communicate, and position this hidden gem in a way that is compelling, accessible, and actionable."

The birthday launch was an attempt to solve this with visual storytelling. We explored comic strips as a format. After honest reflection, I want to make a different argument — one that I believe will serve you better, and one I can demonstrate rather than just describe.

The format argument

Data visualisation is
visual storytelling.

For your specific audience — central bank officials, finance ministers, institutional investors — the most compelling visual is not an illustrated character. It is a number rendered beautifully, a risk score shown clearly, a trend made undeniable.

Your audience is trained to read data. They are suspicious of anything that feels like marketing. What earns their trust is precisely what your research produces — specific numbers, grounded models, named indicators. The visual challenge is not to make that less technical. It is to make it unmissable.

Comic strips offer
Charm

Accessible, warm, human — but signals "this is for general audiences" to senior policy professionals.

Data visualisation offers
Authority

The CRI score rendered as a gauge. GDP-at-risk as a scenario chart. These signal institutional credibility.

Comic strips require
Manual
creation

Each piece is a bespoke production. One strip, one moment. Does not compound over time.

Data visualisation enables
System

Each post is generated from live data. The system compounds — every post builds Rabia's presence automatically.

What I am proposing is not a one-off design project. It is infrastructure — a publishing engine that runs every weekday, grounded in your research, speaking in your voice, reaching your audience without requiring your team to produce content manually.

The capability

An intelligence engine
built on your five years.

I have already built this for another client — a live, automated LinkedIn content pipeline that pulls intelligence from curated sources, reasons through it using AI, and publishes a daily branded post with a data visualisation card. It is running now. You can see it.

For Rabia, the same architecture becomes an intelligence publishing engine grounded in your frameworks. Every morning, the system scans global climate finance news — NGFS publications, BIS working papers, IMF climate notes, Carbon Brief, African Development Bank updates, regional policy developments. It scores those signals against the relevance of your active research. The most significant signal becomes the day's post — framed through your Climate Risk Indicators, your CTRM outputs, your Just Term Sheet lens.

The AI does not have access to your proprietary models or unpublished data. It works from a curated signal layer — a structured summary of your research that you control and update. Your IP stays behind the curtain. Your insights reach the front.

Every post goes to you for approval before it publishes. Nothing goes live without your sign-off. The system handles the research, the writing, and the visual card. You handle the judgment call.

Infrastructure and hosting

How it is
structured.

Best practice for client projects

The engine lives in a dedicated GitHub repository under San Systems' organisation account — San Systems being the technical solutions division of The San Scribe Pty Ltd that designs and builds the infrastructure for this engagement. A separate repo per client, with Rabia added as a collaborator with read access. This means Rabia can see everything running on their behalf, verify the code, and export the entire system at any time. You are never locked in.

The database (Turso — edge SQLite) and the approval UI (Vercel) run on free tiers during Phase 1 at zero additional cost. API costs — the AI calls and news data sources — are covered within the retainer from Phase 2 onwards. During Phase 1 we use DeepSeek (extremely low cost) so the demo and build phase has negligible running costs. Phase 2 upgrades to Claude (Anthropic API) when the retainer kicks in, giving noticeably better output quality.

GitHub Actions handles all scheduling — no server to maintain, no infrastructure to manage. The pipeline runs on GitHub's infrastructure, which means zero hosting cost and full audit trail of every run.
